## September 18 — Silence should mean stop

**Media:** Text-only post.

**Caption:**

One of the most important ROB design ideas is invisible: silence should mean
stop.

When fresh commands stop arriving, motion should not continue indefinitely on
an old instruction. A layered stop chain can include command timeouts,
controller limits, operator control, and a physical emergency stop. No single
layer replaces careful testing, but each layer gives the system another way to
fail safely.

Safety is not the feature added after a robot works. It is part of deciding what
“works” means.

Why do you think a lost connection should produce a stop instead of a guess?

#OrbitusRobotics #RobotSafety #Engineering #ControlSystems #ROB

## September 20 — A systems hunt

**Media:** `static/images/galleries/2020/display/IMG_0997.HEIC.webp`

**Alt text:** Three-quarter close view of early tracked ROB showing its camera
face, shoulder mechanisms, arm wiring, and tank treads.

**Caption:**

Try a systems hunt with ROB.

Pick one visible part, then follow its dependencies. A camera needs power, a
mount, a data path, software that interprets its signal, and a decision about
what happens when the signal is missing. A motor needs structure, control,
feedback, current limits, and a safe way to stop. Even a cable needs routing,
strain relief, clearance, and a label.

Robotics becomes easier to understand when every part is treated as a member of
a team rather than an isolated gadget.

The least glamorous show preparation often prevents the biggest problems.

Test each subsystem. Label both ends of every important cable. Charge batteries

stop path. Pack the tool that fits the unusual connector. Write down what still
