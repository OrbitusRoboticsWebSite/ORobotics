In 2026, R.O.B. became more expressive, more capable, and much safer to operate.
The robot's articulated arms learned coordinated poses around chess experiments,
its Apple-based control system gained secure pairing and bounded autonomy, and
its new blue-and-green lightsabers turned engineering progress into a character
kids can recognize instantly.

The glowing colors are more than a costume. Green and blue make the two sides
easy to follow while learning about mirrored joints, signals, feedback, and
safe motion. Behind the fun is the same rule that guides every R.O.B. build:
people stay in charge, and physical movement is tested carefully.

See those ideas out in the world in the [Maker Faire 2026 gallery]({{< relref "/MakerFaire/MakerFaire_2026" >}}),
with booth photos, droid encounters, and Kierie's footage from the exhibition hall.

Back in the workshop on **October 29**, an ordinary Coke can became a useful
test object. Kierie's footage gives us a close look at ROB holding the can at
the table, bringing it in front of its chest, and keeping hold as its torso
turns above the tracked base. The open opposite gripper makes the two arm
positions easy to compare.

The four images below are labeled stills from that footage. The 28-second video
is one continuous, normal-speed excerpt of the torso turn, presented without
audio and with a written description in its caption and transcript controls.
This supervised practice session focuses on holding and turning with the can.
For more of the sequence, watch the
[recycling demonstrations]({{< relref "/recycling" >}}) and the later
[Coke-pouring experiment]({{< relref "/coke-pour" >}}).
